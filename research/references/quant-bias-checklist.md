# Quantitative Bias Checklist — Pre-Publication Gate

Origin: 2026-06-11 mirror test of the Cato $14.5T fiscal-study criticisms against the research repo's own immigration artifacts (4 hits, none politically directional — the drift is per-claim "favorable construction," not lean). First instance + self-audit table: research-repo `notes/quant-bias-checklist.md`.

**When to run:** before committing any memo, claim register entry, or public artifact where a number does argumentative work, a ranking appears, causal language is used ("caused", "refutes", "explains"), or a welfare/decision conclusion is drawn. A violation means *label the move or downgrade the grade*, not necessarily "don't publish."

**The compressed rule:** every load-bearing number carries its **ledger, unit, base, window justification, gross-or-net status, and measurement-vs-model-output label**, and a number an institution produced also carries its **producer, extract date and any instrument change in the window** — and when several constructions are defensible, the *range across constructions* is the headline, never the favorite endpoint.

**Minimum viable claim** (the object the construction items inspect): a claim is viable when a stranger, author absent, can (1) **re-derive it** — referent operationally defined, relation + value + unit + base, derivation tagged `measurement|model|inference` with source pointer or regenerating command, assumption stack named if model — and (2) **kill it** — scope stated (ledger, window, population, regime) so in-scope counterexamples count, plus a named defeater (the observation or check that would falsify/supersede). For reuse add a load rating (grade) and lifecycle identity (id, asserted-at, status — substrate `claim.v1` already carries exactly this half). Tier escalation: descriptive = base tuple; causal adds identifying variation + rival-channel status; welfare/policy adds welfare weights or [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]. Most audit failures are a claim missing its tier's field — a descriptive claim wearing causal clothes, a welfare verdict hiding its weights.

Historical anchors are canonical cases [TRAINING-DATA]; verify specifics before citing externally.

## A. Ledger & framing

1. **Ledger switching / equivocation.** Different welfare objects (group A's welfare, aggregate output, per-capita, federal vs local budgets, incumbent vs entrant) treated as one. Anchor: Cato 2026 headlined $14.5T immigrants-alone; the with-US-born-children $7.9T didn't lead. → *Name the ledger in the claim sentence; put the adjacent ledger's number beside the headline.*
2. **Welfare weights hidden.** "Good/bad overall" flips with whose welfare counts; the choice is normative. → *State weights or tag [FRAMING-SENSITIVE].*
3. **Per-what mismatch.** Per-person/household/worker/resident denominators flip signs on the same data. Anchor: Heritage per-household vs NAS per-person; household-weighting correction reordered school-burden rankings (Evidence: research-repo `immigration-household-weighted-correction.md`). → *State the unit; check the source's unit before reuse.*

## B. Counterfactual discipline

4. **Frozen-world counterfactual.** "Without X, outcome = Y" with all behavior, prices, and policy held fixed. Anchors: Cato "debt would be 205% of GDP without immigrants"; the research repo's deportation sim holds replacement hiring at zero. → *List the margins held fixed, or label "mechanical calibration, not forecast."*
5. **Policy endogeneity / Lucas-Goodhart.** Estimates from one regime quoted into another; targeted metrics degrade. Anchor: 1970s Phillips-curve breakdown [TRAINING-DATA]. → *State the identifying variation and refuse extrapolation outside it.*
6. **Static-vs-dynamic accounting conflation.** One-period cash flow and lifetime NPV answer different questions; each is "right" only for its question. → *Say which question the accounting answers.*

## C. Windows, bases, denominators

7. **Window selection.** Start/end dates can manufacture the result. Anchors: Reinhart-Rogoff 90%-debt threshold dissolving under Herndon re-analysis [TRAINING-DATA]; 1998-anchored warming "pause" [TRAINING-DATA]; Cato's 1994-2023 = fastest foreign-born growth window (taxes in-window, entitlement draw outside). → *Justify the window by data constraint explicitly; show one alternative window or say why none exists.*
8. **Low-base percentage.** Big % growth from a small base reads as an explosion. Evidence: research-repo CHNV "+787%" from a 2,598/month base — base present in memo, dropped in the summary layer. → *Every % change carries its absolute base inline.*
9. **Endogenous denominator.** If the denominator responds to treatment, ratios mask load (shelter utilization ratios flat while counts explode). Evidence: research-repo `immigration-receiver-counterfactuals-2026-04-22.md` (Chicago; immigration-research, deleted 2026-09-29, recover with `git show 080ddae:research/<file>`). → *Counts beside ratios whenever the denominator can expand under treatment.*
10. **Gross-for-net substitution.** Gross outlays quoted where the question is net burden (reimbursements, offsets, baseline-anyway spending ignored). Evidence: receiver-city cost table = gross city outlays, federal reimbursements unnetted. → *Say gross or net in the claim; if gross, name the known offsets.*

## D. Aggregation & composition

11. **Simpson's paradox / composition shift.** Aggregate trend reverses within every stratum. Anchors: Berkeley 1973 admissions; pandemic average wages "rising" as low-wage workers exited the denominator [TRAINING-DATA]. → *Check the claim within major strata before publishing the aggregate.*
12. **Ecological fallacy.** Area-level correlation read as person-level fact. Anchor: Robinson 1950 [TRAINING-DATA]. → *Match inference level to data level.*
13. **Average hiding the gradient.** A favorable mean produced by one tail (MI: average immigrant +$10K while subgroups span −$400K to +$1M). → *Report the gradient whenever subgroups plausibly differ in sign.*

## E. Model-output laundering

14. **Model output presented as measurement.** A scalar that exists only inside one assumption stack quoted as observed fact. Anchor: the same 22-year-old HS dropout = −$315K or +$45K under five defensible assumption changes (MI vs Cato WP82). Anchor: Flaxman et al. 2020's model credits one public-events ban with 71% of Sweden's R_t reduction and under 2% of other countries', because the country-specific effect sits on whichever intervention came last (Soltesz et al. 2020; agent-infra politicized-statistics memo). → *Headline the sensitivity range or don't headline the scalar; when a model divides credit among policies, name the parameterization that assigns it.*
15. **Amplifier add-ons in the preferred direction.** Second-order modeled effects stacked onto first-order estimates, sum headlined. Anchors: Cato's $3.9T interest savings (27% of headline); research-repo 1.6× Type-II multiplier turning $1.45T into $2.32T. The same fault with no preferred direction: a sum or ratio over terms that overlap, are bounds, cover different populations or are transfers (research-repo 2026-09-21: "offsets ten times too small" was built from a term already inside the headline, one overlapping it, an upper bound and another population's transfer). → *Lead with first-order; amplified figures only as labeled sensitivity; sum only disjoint terms on one ledger, otherwise state the threshold each would have to reach.*
16. **Upper-bound laundering.** Stylized maxima quoted as realistic baselines ("double world GDP"). → *Tag bounds as bounds.*
17. **Shared-cost attribution.** Average-cost vs marginal-cost allocation of public goods/overhead is routinely the single largest swing factor and flips signs (NAS scenarios; in a deficit entity, marginal-cost attribution makes any contributor an "asset"). → *Run or cite both attributions; never present one as "what happened."*

## F. Selection & measurement

18. **Survivorship / attrition.** The sample is the survivors. Anchors: Wald's WWII bomber armor; Secrist 1933 "triumph of mediocrity" [TRAINING-DATA]. → *Ask who exited the sample and why.*
19. **Sample-frame selection.** Anchor: Literary Digest 1936 — 2.4M responses, off by 19 points; N does not cure frame bias [TRAINING-DATA]. → *State the frame.*
20. **Collider conditioning.** Conditioning on a downstream consequence manufactures correlation. Anchor: Berkson's paradox [TRAINING-DATA]; research-repo DAG rule: never condition on apprehension or appearing-in-admin-data. → *Check controls against the DAG; more controls is not monotonically better.*
21. **Construct mismatch.** The measured variable is not the rhetorical category (e.g., "low-skill" operationalized as less-than-HS ≠ "non-college"). → *Define the operational variable next to every category word.*
22. **Silent proxy for the principal.** Exposure/stress proxies read as the welfare object itself (rent exposure ≠ housing welfare loss). → *A proxy is a labeled screen, never the verdict.* (See agent-infra `decisions/2026-06-10-silent-proxy-as-truth.md` for the general rule.)

## G. Inference discipline

23. **Garden of forking paths / p-hacking.** Many undisclosed analyst choices, one published path. Anchors: Simmons et al. 2011; Wansink retractions [TRAINING-DATA]. Evidence: research-repo 2026-09-21, three memos omitted specifications their own scripts had computed (a full-sample fit, an alternative regressor, a second cost allocation); a reader who finds the unreported row in the outputs reads it as suppressed. → *Pre-register the decision rule (/verify-before preregister); disclose paths tried; report every specification the code computed, or say in a clause why one is not preferred.*
24. **Multiple comparisons.** Anchor: dead-salmon fMRI [TRAINING-DATA]. Positive pattern: 1,000-draw permutation inference (research-repo capacity-falsification pass). → *Permutation/null benchmarks for any scan over many cells.*
25. **Plausibility assertion doing causal work.** "Too large to be anything else" substituting for decomposition. Evidence: research-repo "+4.4pp swing implausibly large for non-immigration causes" — asserted over a near-collinear confound (most-Hispanic counties during a national Hispanic realignment), regression lacking the Hispanic-share control. → *Quantify the rival channel or mark [INFERENCE] and downgrade.*
26. **Spurious time-series correlation.** Trending/nonstationary series correlate by construction. Anchor: Yule 1926 [TRAINING-DATA]. → *Difference, detrend, or design (event study) before claiming co-movement.*
27. **Extrapolation beyond support.** Marginal evidence answering mass-regime questions; in-sample fit answering out-of-distribution policy. Anchors: LTCM 1998; "national house prices never fall" 2008 [TRAINING-DATA]. → *State the support region in the claim itself.*

## H. Publication & process

28. **Headline selection among defensible constructions.** When several constructions are defensible, choosing the favorite as the lead IS the bias — both sides of the Cato dispute did it from the same NAS-family machinery. → *Lead with the range across constructions.*
29. **Advocacy-source laundering.** Numbers acquire false neutrality through citation chains (advocacy-org constructions misattributed to the National Academies). → *Carry provenance tags through every reuse; check the original.*
30. **Reviewer-direction asymmetry.** Hostile audits only for uncongenial conclusions. Mirror-test pattern: audit your own work against the *specific* failure list you just compiled for an adversary — generic "be rigorous" finds nothing; a named list found 4 real hits in one session. → *Run the same-intensity audit on the congenial result (blind first-pass; steel-man both).*
31. **Publication/replication base rates.** Most published effects shrink on replication (Ioannidis 2005; OSC 2015 [TRAINING-DATA]). The same selection runs inside a summary: results ranked by surprise, or by how far they contradict the writer's prior, are the ones most likely to be wrong, and a model correcting for its training lean can overshoot (research-repo 2026-09-21: an agent's "most surprising given pre-training" ranking promoted the entries its own audit then walked back). → *Citation-stance checks (scite contrast); one paper is a hypothesis, not a finding; the more surprising a result, the more checking it gets before it leads a summary.*
32. **Unlabeled adversarial artifacts.** One-sided briefs are legitimate only when labeled. → *Every artifact states its mode: estimate, bound, screen, or brief.*
33. **Unanchored derived series.** A parsed/derived time series regressed on without checking it against a single externally published value. Evidence: research-repo OHSS parser carried fiscal-index dates AND an agency-block overwrite (series was ports-only, ~5x low) for 7 weeks across 3 ladder claims — one anchor check (Dec 2023 SWB ≈ 302K) caught both in minutes, and the headline finding reversed (2026-06-11 decision record). → *Before the first regression on any built series: verify one known published value, and eyeball one known event (a peak, a collapse) at its calendar date.*

## I. Instrument & institution

Items 1–33 inspect how an author builds a number. These inspect who controls whether and how the number exists: the instrument, the definition, the series, the extract date, the consensus, the retraction, the data access and the report. **Run this section** when an institution produced the number (statistical agency, health department, court filing, journal, commissioned report) or when the claim is about consensus or trust; for an author-built number, items 1–33 suffice. For a statistic built from administrative or survey records, the `/analyze` measurement-pipeline lens walks the production stages first; check its table against these items. Anchors are grounded on primary documents, with cases sought in both political directions, in agent-infra `research/2026-09-16-politicized-statistics-mechanisms-2016-2026.md`; re-run that memo's anchor probe before citing one externally.

34. **Instrument change read as trend.** A new form, rule, coverage or definition moves the number while the events stay put. Anchors: US maternal mortality rose 144% under the death-certificate pregnancy checkbox, while a coding of the same records that does not rely on the checkbox moved from 10.2 to 10.4 per 100,000; the FBI's 2021 switch to NIBRS, for which the FBI declined to publish a 2020–2021 change; the charge that CDC "counted the vaccinated as unvaccinated" is false for CDC's national product, which excluded the partially vaccinated from numerator and denominator, and true for Utah's dashboard, which filed them under "Unvaccinated". → *List every instrument change inside the window and show the series under both rules; quote each producer's own definition before judging a claim about it; do not cite a change the producer declined to publish.*
35. **Series discontinuation, removal or status change.** Pausing, retiring, deleting or de-accrediting a series is an institutional act, and the gap is a finding in itself. Anchors: China's National Bureau of Statistics paused youth unemployment at 21.3% and resumed five months later on a revised definition at 14.9%, with no back-cast; NOAA retired its billion-dollar disaster product in May 2025; after Labour Force Survey response rates fell, the UK regulator changed the accreditation of the affected statistics, while figures from the warned period were quoted in Parliament. → *Date the gap, name who decided and the stated reason, and look for a bridge or back-cast; do not compare across the break without one; before quoting a series, check the producer's quality status for the quoted period.*
36. **Extract date is a variable.** Administrative counts are revised as records arrive, so one period has several published values. Anchors: the FBI's 2022 violent-crime change was −2.1% at first release and +4.5% after revision, without a flag; the BLS payroll benchmark for March 2024 was −818,000 preliminary and −598,000 final, and the March 2025 benchmark of −898,000 was published after the commissioner's removal. → *Carry the extract or vintage date with every administrative number; compare like vintage with like; treat a revision as the instrument's documented behavior until shown otherwise; a revision that runs against the producer's interest is evidence against capture.*
37. **Residual category renamed as a group.** The unknown, unmatched or imputed bucket receives a group's name, so classification failure is counted as membership. Anchor: Utah's COVID dashboard filed partially vaccinated people under "Unvaccinated". Item 21 covers the general gap between a label and the measured variable. → *Find the residual, unknown or imputed category, who named it, and what share of it is classification failure; read the denominator definition, not the category label.*
38. **Statistical-agency interference.** Pressure on the producer is part of a number's provenance: a dismissal, a prosecution, a question added for a purpose other than the one stated. Anchors: the BLS commissioner removed on 1 August 2025 after a downward revision; the census citizenship question, whose sole stated reason the Supreme Court found "contrived"; Greece's former chief statistician prosecuted over a 2009 deficit figure that Eurostat validated and defended in writing. → *Record the producer's independence status with the number; test a manipulation claim against releases produced after the intervention; ask whoever alleges manipulation for their competing construction, and record whether one exists.*
39. **Selective release by the producer.** A producer that holds back a cut because it "might be misinterpreted" has applied a directional filter. Anchors: CDC withheld booster data for adults aged 18–49 on that stated reason, as entered in a House hearing record; public health officials in Scotland stopped releasing hospitalization and death data by vaccination status for the same stated reason. → *Ask what the producer holds and did not publish, and quote its stated reason beside the gap.*
40. **Consensus claims and signature letters.** A consensus percentage or a signature count stands in for a measurement it does not contain. Anchors: the "97%" consensus is 97.1% of the 32.6% of abstracts that took a position, with 66.4% taking none; duelling economists' letters on the Inflation Reduction Act (126 signatories on one side, 230 and later 369 on the other, Nobel laureates on both) stated no magnitude, horizon or counterfactual; the Proximal Origin paper's conclusion against its authors' contemporaneous Slack messages and later sworn testimony; the Lancet's February 2020 statement, whose claims stand after a 2021 competing-interests addendum. → *Quote the denominator of any consensus percentage; check whether the statement names a magnitude, horizon and counterfactual, and give a signature count no evidential weight if it does not; compare the public statement with the signatories' released private record; check whether a later correction or disclosure changed the claim itself.*
41. **Scenario as baseline.** A conditional scenario is quoted as the expected path, or as the counterfactual behind a "lives saved" claim. Anchors: Imperial College Report 9's unmitigated projection, which its authors labelled "(unlikely)", while its own Table 4 spans 8,700 to 510,000 deaths in Great Britain across scenarios (58.6×); RCP8.5 cited as business as usual. Items 4 and 16 cover frozen-world counterfactuals and bounds quoted as baselines. → *Quote the authors' conditional and likelihood label next to the number; when a scenario is the counterfactual for a credit claim, say so and show the scenario range.*
42. **Retraction reason as evidence.** The stated reason for a correction or retraction is data; a political reading of it is not. Anchor: the PNAS police-shooting paper was corrected in April 2020 for a named error, estimating P(race | shot) where its claim needed P(shot | race), then retracted in July 2020 "given continued misuse", with the authors denying political motive in writing. → *Quote the stated reason verbatim; separate an error-driven correction from a citation-driven escalation; a retraction without a named error is no evidence against the finding, and one with a named error is no evidence of persecution.*
43. **Communication number vs estimand.** A number chosen for its effect on the audience is not the speaker's estimate. Anchors: the herd-immunity threshold stated in public moved from 70% toward 85% as polled vaccine willingness rose ("I'm not going to say 90%"); "I wanted to always play it down", on pandemic severity, recorded on 19 March 2020. → *Find the speaker's own estimate or interval in their technical or on-record statements, quote it beside the public number, and label the public number as communication.*
44. **Evidence-hierarchy asymmetry.** One evidence standard is applied strictly to an unwelcome conclusion and loosely to a welcome one, or one grading is rendered two ways. Anchors: in the most heavily weighted trial of the 2023 Cochrane mask review, 42.3% of the intervention arm and 13.3% of the control arm wore masks, so the trials estimate the effect of promoting masks rather than wearing them, and the review's promised correction of its summary was abandoned in June 2024; the York reviews behind the Cass Review rated studies with a modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, not GRADE, and "98% not rated high quality" and "nearly 60% synthesised" are both true of the same 103 studies, while "around 100 studies have not been included", said in the Commons on 15 April 2024, is false against the counts. → *Hold both directions to one standard in the same paragraph; name the estimand the trials identify; do not slide between certainty in an effect (GRADE) and the quality of a study (Newcastle-Ottawa); when two sides quote different percentages of one body of evidence, name each predicate; check which review a policy actually cites, and its date.*
45. **Data-access refusal.** A finding whose data an interested party will not release cannot be replicated, and a statute can make the refusal durable. Anchors: McKinsey's diversity-and-performance series did not replicate on public data (p = 0.65, null on six measures), and the replicators report that "McKinsey would not provide us with their detailed datasets"; the Tiahrt amendments restrict release of federal firearm trace data; FDA proposed releasing the Pfizer vaccine trial records at a floor of 500 pages a month, the court ordered 55,000 pages every 30 days, and the widely quoted "75 years" appears in neither filing. → *Treat the claim as a brief (item 32), not a measurement, until the data are available; check whether a statute or court governs release, and quote its terms rather than a derived horizon.*
46. **Litigation-grade statistics.** A statistic filed in court carries the null hypothesis and independence assumptions its author chose. Anchor: a declaration filed with Texas's December 2020 Supreme Court motion put the chance of the reported result at "less than one in a quadrillion", against a null that Biden's 2020 vote would equal Clinton's 2016 vote, with probabilities from dependent tests raised to the fourth power. → *State the null the probability rejects and whether it is the question at issue; check that multiplied probabilities come from independent tests.*
47. **Definitional expansion.** Widening or narrowing a definition moves the count and the ranking while the headline keeps the same word. Anchors: firearms rank first among causes of death for ages 1–19, second for 1–17, and not first for 0–17; mass-shooting databases differ 31.5× for 2017 (346 vs 11) and still 4.8× after harmonizing definitions (24 vs 5); in ADL's 2023 audit, 26% of the record rise came from definition changes and a 65% rise survives the strictest cut. → *Print the operational definition next to the count, and the count under the narrowest defensible definition; when the producer publishes every cut, record which one was quoted.*
48. **Post-publication criteria at journals and agencies.** A criterion other than evidence, applied to already-published work, changes who decides what stays in the record, not what is true. Anchors: Nature Human Behaviour's 2022 editorial criterion of potential harm to human groups; federal health pages and datasets removed under Executive Order 14168 and restored under court order (Doctors for America v. OPM). → *Record the criterion, who applied it, and whether an outside appeal or review channel exists; when the criterion removes data, also apply item 35.*
49. **Outside text in the file.** Text written by an interested outside party enters an agency's guidance or rule. Anchors: CDC's February 2021 school guidance kept 96.7% of the words of a teachers' union's first edit, rearranged so that none of its four sentences survived intact, and neither union edit contained a closure trigger; a 2018 offshore well-control rulemaking incorporates American Petroleum Institute standards by reference, which is lawful and attributed but leaves the operative text copyrighted and unprinted. → *Diff the document against the outside party's text when both are available; when text is incorporated by reference, say who wrote it and that readers cannot read it in the rule.*
50. **Instrument whose owner is its only auditor.** A dashboard, a platform's error rate or a report revised in place is checked only by the party that runs it. Anchors: Hamilton 68's own methodology said its 600 tracked accounts were "not all … directly controlled by Russia", and the list was never published; Meta ended US third-party fact-checking in January 2025, and the only number it offered was its own estimate that one to two in ten enforcement actions may be mistakes. → *State the instrument's own caveats beside any use of it and whether an outside party can audit it; do not upgrade "misused" to "fabricated" without a named fabrication.*
51. **Report built to a conclusion.** A commissioned report's roster, timeline and review process can predict its conclusion. Anchors: the 2025 MAHA report had 7 nonexistent and at least 18 altered citations among 522 footnotes and was revised in place without notice; the Department of Energy Climate Working Group report came from five authors in about four months with internal review only, and some of its claims still match the mainstream literature. Both anchors come from one administration; no case from the other political direction has been grounded. → *Record the author roster, drafting timeline, review status and correction practice; spot-check a sample of load-bearing citations for existence and quotation accuracy; judge each claim on its evidence, since a predictable report can still be right.*
52. **Trust as an outcome.** A partisan trust series flips sign with control of the executive, so it cannot identify a politicization event on its own. Anchors: Pew found Republican confidence in scientists at 85% in April 2020 and 66% in October 2024; Gallup's 2025 nine-institution average put Republicans at 37% and Democrats at 26%, the largest party gap in 46 years, while independents held at 25%. → *Name the instrument (confidence in scientists, confidence in institutions, perceived motives); use independents as the control; compare across a change of administration before attributing a shift to an event.*

## Mechanically checkable subset (hook candidates on recurrence)

Items 1 (ledger named near headline), 8 (base near any %), 14 (sensitivity range present when "model"/"scenario" appears), 15 (multiplier/amplifier keywords inside headline ranges), 29 (provenance tag present on reused scalars), 34 (an instrument-change line present when a trend is claimed), 36 (an extract or vintage date present near any administrative number). Don't build the hook until a project shows repeat violations post-checklist — instructions first, architecture on recurrence.
